Ikou user testing: where learners struggled

6 testers, 4 Nov 2026. Everyone liked the quests. The trouble was in getting started: knowing what to press and what Japanese was being taught.

The quests were fun for everyone; onboarding and navigation were the soft spots

Answers to four statements, one block per tester

YesKind ofNo

What it shows. All 6 said the quests were fun and engaging. Each of the other three statements got 4 yeses. Every “Kind of” or “No” was about onboarding, instructions or navigation. The one outright “No” was on whether the app’s purpose was clear at the start. Everyone said they’d come back (3 very likely, 3 likely).

What 6 testers can’t tell us. Whether “4 of 6” would come out as 67% for a bigger group. One more unhappy tester would move it to 57%, so read these as signs, not rates. The scale also stops at 3 answers and nobody was offered “unlikely” to return. On top of that, testers who know the team tend to be kind, so “fun” is probably an upper bound.

The friction came from the less experienced testers

Each tester’s answers, sorted by how long they have studied Japanese

YesKind ofNo

What it shows. Only two testers gave anything below “Yes”. T6 has never studied Japanese and T2 has studied for 1–2 years. Both also asked for more instructions in their comments. The two testers with 5+ years said yes to everything. Absolute beginners are the app’s audience, so this is the group whose onboarding matters most.

What 6 testers can’t tell us. Whether experience causes the friction. This is 2 people, and T3, another non-studier, said yes to everything. All 6 had been to Japan, so we can’t tell how that affects things, and it means none of them is a typical newcomer. Each experience level holds only 1–2 people.

What testers would change: instructions, teaching, and controls

Testers whose “what would you change / improve” answers mention each theme (5 of 6 answered)

What it shows. Three themes tie at 3 testers each. (1) Clearer instructions that can be read again, including that Space advances the dialog. (2) Teaching the words before the quest asks for them: a lesson, a vocab list, definitions. (3) Faster movement and more control keys. The second matters most for the learning goal: T2 said it wasn’t clear what was being taught to a beginner.

What 6 testers can’t tell us. How these rank against each other. A 3–3–3 tie among 5 people is noise. One person coded these themes from short free-text answers, and someone else might group them differently. Testers also only mention what comes to mind, so leaving a theme out doesn’t mean they were happy with it.
